# How to play Long Haul

## The goal

Deliver the load and make money doing it. Every trip is paid by the pound
when it reaches the dock, and every trip costs: diesel, tolls, tyres, fines,
motel rooms, and $85 a day for the truck payment, insurance and taxes. What
is left is your profit. Get there too slowly and freight is late (10 % off)
or the oranges start to spoil; drive too fast and the patrols, the tyres and
the road itself will make you pay. Crash, and you lose the truck and the
trip; a fourth offence costs you your licence.

## Your first trip

1. Choose **Single Haul** on the title screen. You are at the Los Angeles
   terminal, Monday 8 AM, with 190 gallons in the tank.
2. Pick a **cargo**. *Oranges* pay best (6.5 ¢ a pound) if they arrive within
   about four days and the refrigeration unit behaves; *freight* pays 5 ¢ and
   is due Thursday 4 PM, with 10 % off from Friday 7 AM; *U.S. Mail* pays
   4.75 ¢ whenever it gets there. For a first trip, take oranges.
3. Set the **weight**. The sheet tells you how much is legal at the scales
   with the fuel you carry; 38,000 lb is a good start.
4. Decide about the **two worn tyres**. Retreads ($100 each) or new tyres
   ($200) make blowouts rarer; you carry one spare either way.
5. Choose a **route** on the map. The cards compare distance, tolls, scales,
   work zones, radar spots, time zones, how strict the patrols are, how hard
   the road is on tyres, and the weather. The middle route is a fair start.
6. **Hit the road.** Keep the throttle near 55: that is where the rig gets
   its best mileage, and well under the patrols' threshold.
7. Watch the **route strip** at the top. The amber tick is the next waypoint,
   the fuel-pump mark the next truck stop; on the right are the miles to go,
   your ETA and the due time, in green, amber or red.
8. When a **truck stop** sign slides in, press **T** to pull in. Fill the tank,
   and if the driver is tired and it is evening, sleep eight hours in the
   bunk. If you would rather not stop, drive on; but the game will stop and
   ask if the tank is low, you are tired, or the spare is gone.
9. Read the **event cards** as they come: a blowout, a patrol car, a scale,
   a work zone. Press Continue (or A) to drive on.
10. At the dock, the **logbook** shows your trip, the receipts add up, and the
    original's stamp gives its verdict.

## Modes

### Single Haul

The original run, Los Angeles to New York on one of three roads, or the
**return trip** New York to Los Angeles: same roads, reversed, with the
clocks going back three hours. Your record carries from trip to trip: the
running total of profit, your average, and your offences. Lose money with a
running total below zero and the rig is repossessed, as in the original.

### Career

An old rig, $1,000 in the bank and a name nobody knows, in Los Angeles,
March 1982. Every morning the **job board** at your terminal posts five
loads to other cities, with the pay, the weight, the distance and the due
time; some are *rush* (sooner, better paid), *heavy* (more pay, but legal at
the scales only with a light tank: the board tells you how many gallons) or
carry an *early bonus* (10 % more if you beat the due time).

- **Profit carries over**, and so does the diesel left in your tank.
- **Reputation** rises with on-time deliveries and falls with late, spoiled
  or lost loads. A good name brings longer, better-paid runs.
- **The garage** sells six upgrades: saddle tanks (300 gallons), a fuel-saver
  engine (15 % less diesel), a sleeper cab (daytime sleep counts in full), a
  reliable reefer unit, radial tyres, and a radar detector (illegal in
  Virginia and Washington, D.C.).
- **Wait a day** for a new board: the truck payment is due either way.
- **Seasons** turn: blizzards in the Rockies in winter, Gulf rain in spring.
- A **wreck** costs a $500 deductible, three days in the shop and the load,
  but not the career. A **fourth offence** within sixty days ends it, and so
  does a bank balance below zero. You can also **retire** whenever you like
  and see how the career went.

### Daily Haul

One load a day, the same for everyone: the same cities, cargo, weather and
luck. Your first run of the day counts; after that you can drive it again
for practice. A calendar shows the days you have driven and a streak counts
them. The **Share** button copies a line with your profit and time, never
your road. A new load arrives at midnight UTC. The Daily Haul is always on
Normal difficulty.

### Postcards

Pass a town for the first time and its postcard goes in your album; cross
into a state and you get its welcome card. Open a card to read the back.

## Settings

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Play rhythm** | *Real-time*: each hour plays in about three seconds; the throttle sets the speed. *Leg by leg*: choose a speed at each waypoint (and when the weather or your fatigue changes), then the leg plays out quickly, and every truck stop asks. |
| **View** | *Side view* (the diorama) or *Cab*. You can switch at any time with V. |
| **Real-time speed** | 1×, 2× or 4×: how fast the hours go by in real time. |
| **Stop the clock for** | *Serious events* (crashes, police, blowouts, scales, running dry) or *every event*. |
| **Difficulty** | See below. A trip keeps the difficulty it started with. |
| **Units** | Miles or kilometres, for display. |
| **Theme** | Follow the device, day or night. |
| **Text mode (the original)** | Plays Single Haul as the original did: green text on black, an hour at a time. Type a speed and press Enter (Enter alone repeats the last one); answer Y or N with a key. |
| **Rig paint** | Five colour schemes beyond the classic red, unlocked on the Arcade Pass. They change nothing but the colours. |
| **Sound** | Volume, music and effects. The mix is shared by every game in the arcade. |

## How the money works

- **Pay**: oranges 6.5 ¢, freight 5 ¢, mail 4.75 ¢ a pound for the original run;
  career and daily contracts pay the same rates scaled by distance, and say
  so on the board.
- **Oranges**: each point of damage (a reefer failure, a dry tank while the
  reefer runs) takes 5 % off; from the fifth day on the road the load picks
  up more damage; over six points it is hauled to the dump.
- **Freight**: 10 % off if it reaches the dock after the cutoff (Friday 7 AM
  on the original run).
- **The dock** is closed from 6 PM to 6 AM; a night arrival waits.
- **Costs**: diesel (85 ¢ to $1.19 a gallon at the stops), tolls, tyres,
  speeding and overweight fines, motel rooms, coffee, the tow truck ($400
  when a tyre blows without a spare), a barrel of diesel brought to the
  roadside ($200 when you run dry), and $85 for every day started.
- **The verdict**: "G O O D   W O R K  !!" over $100 profit; "You'd make more
  money washing dishes !" under $200 or with an average under $250.

## The road

- **Speed**: the rig manages half again the limit at most. Fuel economy is
  best at 55 mph and falls away on either side.
- **Patrols** start to care above the limit plus ten, minus a margin that
  depends on the route (strict in the north, easygoing in the south).
  Each offence costs hours at the justice of the peace and a fine; the
  fourth is jail.
- **Radar traps** are marked on the route strip with an R, scales with a W,
  work zones with a warning sign and tolls with a $. The CB will often tell
  you which are active today.
- **Weigh stations** fine every pound over 60,000 gross: the rig, the cargo,
  7 lb for every gallon of diesel and your own weight. Louisiana turns
  overweight trucks back for a 200-mile detour.
- **Fatigue**: rested, fine, bored, tired, fatigued, exhausted. The more
  tired, the likelier a crash, especially at speed and in bad weather. Sleep
  more than three hours to start fresh; a coffee helps for an hour or two.
  Daytime sleep in the bunk only half counts; a motel room or a sleeper cab
  is quiet.
- **Weather**: clear, wet, rain, light snow, fog, blizzard. On the original run it
  gets worse further east; elsewhere, storms drift across the map, and the
  route planner shows the forecast.
- **Refrigerated loads** run the reefer on diesel: 7 gallons an hour while
  you are parked. Do not let it run dry.

## Tips and tricks

- **55 is the magic number**: the best mileage, nowhere near a ticket.
- **Plan the night**: aim to reach a truck stop around 9 or 10 PM and sleep
  eight hours. Sleeping at noon in the bunk wastes half of it.
- **Do not pass a stop with less than a third of a tank**: the next one may
  be four hours away, and a barrel at the roadside costs $200.
- **Buy a spare** at a stop as soon as you have used yours: a second blowout
  without one costs $400 and four hours.
- **Freight wants hustle**: 80 hours for 2,850 miles means few long sleeps.
  If you are a careful driver, haul oranges or mail.
- **Watch the scales**: with a heavy load, carry less fuel through them.
- **Listen to the CB**, but learn who is reliable. Mama Bear and Old Timer
  are rarely wrong.
- **Slow down for the R on the strip.** A radar reading three over the limit
  is a ticket.

## FAQ

**Is this the same game as the original?** The rules are, number for number,
with its bugs fixed (listed in the
[behaviour notes](../../../docs/games/long-haul.md)). Set *Text mode* in the
settings to play it as it looked.

**Why did the program stop and ask me to pull in?** In real time a truck
stop goes by unless you press T. If your tank is under 30 %, you are tired
or you have no spare, the game stops and asks, as the original asked at
every stop.

**Why was my freight late when I arrived on time?** The dock is closed from
6 PM to 6 AM. A late-evening arrival waits for the morning, and the strip's
deadline colour already counts that wait.

**Can I save in the middle of a trip?** Not yet: a trip runs from terminal to
dock. Careers, records, postcards and daily results are saved between trips.

**Does the paint on my rig change anything?** No. Paints are cosmetic.

**Why do I keep crashing at 70 mph?** The crash risk grows with the square of
your speed, times your fatigue and the weather. At 70 in the rain while
tired, it is many times what it is at 55 on a clear morning.
